32:T1b2a,<p>You open Search Console and see two ugly words: &quot;Crawled, currently not indexed.&quot; Google crawled but not indexed your page. It visited. It looked around. Then it left without saving anything.</p>
<p>That means your page can&#39;t show up in search. No index, no traffic.</p>
<p>Don&#39;t panic. This is one of the most common Google Search Console indexing issues, and most of the time you can fix it. Think of your page like a patient at a clinic. We run a quick checkup, find the problem, and give the right treatment.</p>
<p>Here are 7 fixes that work in 2026.</p>
<h2 id="1-know-what-google-crawled-but-not-indexed-really-means">1. Know What &quot;Google Crawled but Not Indexed&quot; Really Means</h2>
<p>Crawling and indexing are two different steps.</p>
<p>Crawling means Google&#39;s bot visited your page. Indexing means Google decided the page is good enough to store and show in results.</p>
<p>So this status is not an error. It&#39;s a decision. Google saw your page and said, &quot;Not yet.&quot; Your job is to give Google a reason to change its mind.</p>
<p>Also note that this is different from &quot;Discovered, currently not indexed.&quot; In that case Google hasn&#39;t even visited the page yet.</p>
<h2 id="2-confirm-the-page-is-really-not-indexed">2. Confirm the Page Is Really Not Indexed</h2>
<p>Before you fix anything, check the facts. Search Console data can lag by a few days.</p>
<ul>
<li>Paste the URL into the <strong>URL Inspection tool</strong> and read the live status.</li>
<li>Search <code>site:yourdomain.com/page-url</code> on Google.</li>
<li>Check the last crawl date. If it&#39;s very old, the data may be stale.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the page shows up in these checks, you&#39;re fine. If not, move on to the next fixes.</p>
<h2 id="3-fix-thin-or-copied-content">3. Fix Thin or Copied Content</h2>
<p>This is the number one reason a page is not indexed by Google.</p>
<p>Google has billions of pages to store. It skips pages that add nothing new. Watch for these signs:</p>
<ul>
<li>Under 300 words with no real answer</li>
<li>Text that is copied or lightly reworded from other sites</li>
<li>Many pages that say almost the same thing</li>
<li>AI written pages with no real experience or facts</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask one simple question: &quot;Would someone learn something here that they can&#39;t learn on the top 10 results?&quot; If the answer is no, <a href="/services/content-writing">rewrite the page</a>. Add your own examples, data, screenshots and opinions.</p>
<h2 id="4-add-internal-links-from-strong-pages">4. Add Internal Links From Strong Pages</h2>
<p>Google decides how important a page is by how your site links to it. A page with no links is like a shop in a dead end street.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s what to do:</p>
<ul>
<li>Link to the page from your homepage or a top blog post</li>
<li>Use clear anchor text, not &quot;click here&quot;</li>
<li>Add the page to a relevant category or hub page</li>
<li>Link to 3 to 5 related pages from inside it</li>
</ul>
<p><a href="/services/seo-aeo-services">Strong internal linking for SEO</a> is often the cheapest fix on this list. It costs nothing and works fast.</p>
<h2 id="5-check-noindex-and-canonical-tags">5. Check Noindex and Canonical Tags</h2>
<p>Sometimes you did this to yourself. A small code mistake can block a page.</p>
<p>Open your page source and look for:</p>
<ul>
<li>A <code>noindex</code> tag in the robots meta tag</li>
<li>A canonical tag that points to a different URL</li>
<li>A redirect chain that sends Google somewhere else</li>
<li>A page blocked in robots.txt</li>
</ul>
<p>If the canonical points to another page, Google will index that page and skip yours. Make sure each page you want in search has a self canonical tag.</p>
<h2 id="6-clean-up-your-sitemap-and-site-structure">6. Clean Up Your Sitemap and Site Structure</h2>
<p>Your sitemap tells Google what you care about. A messy sitemap sends mixed signals.</p>
<ul>
<li>Keep only indexable pages in the sitemap</li>
<li>Remove redirected, broken or noindex URLs</li>
<li>Update the lastmod date only when you really change the page</li>
<li>Fix orphan pages, which are pages with zero internal links</li>
</ul>
<p>A clean sitemap helps Google spend its crawl budget on pages that matter. This is a big deal for large sites with hundreds of URLs.</p>
<h2 id="7-improve-speed-and-page-experience">7. Improve Speed and Page Experience</h2>
<p>If your page loads slowly or breaks on mobile, Google may not bother to index it.</p>
<p>Quick checks:</p>
<ul>
<li>Run the page through PageSpeed Insights</li>
<li>Make sure the main content shows without heavy JavaScript</li>
<li>Test it on a real phone</li>
<li>Compress big images and remove extra scripts</li>
</ul>
<p>Google can only index what it can render. If your key text loads late or hides behind scripts, Google may see an empty page.</p>

<h2 id="after-the-fix-request-indexing-and-be-patient">After the Fix: Request Indexing and Be Patient</h2>
<p>Once you&#39;ve made changes, go back to the URL Inspection tool and click <strong>Request Indexing</strong>. This tells Google to take another look.</p>
<p>Then wait. Indexing can take a few days to a few weeks. Don&#39;t spam the button. It won&#39;t speed things up.</p>
<p>If a page is still not indexed after 4 to 6 weeks, the problem is almost always content quality. Go back to fix number 3 and be honest about it. Sometimes the right move is to delete a weak page or merge it into a better one.</p>
<h2 id="final-thoughts">Final Thoughts</h2>
<p>When Google crawled but not indexed your page, it&#39;s not a penalty. It&#39;s feedback. Google is telling you the page needs more value, more links or a cleaner setup.</p>
<p>Start with content quality. Then fix links, tags and speed. Request indexing and give it time.</p>
